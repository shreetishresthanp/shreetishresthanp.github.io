#### Summary
The authors introduce a framework, LatentQA, to answer open-ended questions about model activations in natural language to help understand model representations more easily in a human-interpretable form as opposed to vectors/scalars/circuits generated by traditional interpretability methods. They propose Latent Interpretation Tuning (LIT), which finetunes a decoder on a curated dataset of activations and QA labels, to predict qualitative properties of future model completions based on the activations of the current prompt. The key innovation of LatentQA (by way of LIT) in comparison to similar efforts in PatchScope (Ghandeharioun, Asma, et al.) and SelfIE(Chen et al., 2024a) is that they train a decoder instead of directly applying patch activations from one layer of the target LLM to another which resolves the issue of distribution shift (layer X might have different distribution stats compared to layer Y, thus resulting in unreliable brittle results). Key implementation details and findings are listed below:
**Dataset Curation**: Across three different controls: (i) ExtractiveQA(offers context), (ii) Goals (promotes a certain intent), and (iii) Persona (encourages a certain role), the authors generate thousands of examples. Each of these are then fed to OpenAI's o1 preview LLM to expand to a synthetic dialog that contains a control, a simulation and a completion. Then, the LLM is further asked to generate description based QA and reasoning based QA pairs about the control for each of these dialogs. The total dataset includes 16,732 data points.
Key dataset decisions include:
- Masking control activations to avoid "shortcutting" the task (by reading only the token embeddings of the control from the residual stream)
- Data augmentation  to include three data types--control, stimulus, stimulus + completion--to provide coverage for various use cases.
- Improving faithfulness by using a more capable LLM (o1 preview)

**Decoder Training**: The decoder is a copy of the target LLM and is the Llama-3 family of models. This decoder is finetuned using LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64). The activations derived from Layer 15 of the target LLM (since middle layers are semantically rich) is then fed to layer 0 of this decoder LLM, which then handles the distribution shift during training.

**Key Findings**
- **Reading**
	- LIT outperforms linear probes (by an absolute difference of 32.2%) and Patchscope (by an absolute difference of 38.2%) when tested to extract information from representations across 6 tasks.
	- LIT outperforms GPT-4 prompting by 10.8% in decoding information stored in the activations to uncover hidden system prompts
- **Control**
	- LIT is the only control method among the tested which statistically significantly reduces biases in the model
	- Among 4 tested methods, LIT is consistently in the top 2 rank for effectively steering model sentiment
	- LIT trained decoder elicits harmful knowledge from the model which prompting baseline seems to evade
- The LatentQA approach benefits from scaling model size and dataset size.

Through experiments, they confirm that this approach benefits from scaling (both in terms of model size and dataset size) and hence, could unlock new potential for decoding model activations. 

#### Strengths
1. Thoughtful Dataset Design: Their strategy to mask control activations and use three variations (control, stimulus, stimulus + completion) builds a case for comprehensive coverage without shortcutting that hints towards generalizability. 
2. Focus on practical implications: Their testing on reading + control applications (albeit on synthetic data) like understanding relational knowledge, and eliciting harmful knowledge demonstrates that this approach works for actual models and not just toy tasks. Especially since it's scalable, it provides a future direction to test out these hypotheses on more robust datasets.

**Weaknesses**
1. Heavy reliance on o1-preview for data generation: Since they're using one LLM (o1 preview) to generate training data for interpreting another LLM (Llama models), there could be this circular dependency where if o1's QA pairs are incorrect, or biased, then the decoder learns incorrect interpretations or propagates the bias that's coming in.
2. Absence of ground truth validation: The authors acknowledge risk of hallucination due to lack of ground truth, but I think the bigger problem is even if plausible activation interpretations are generated, how do we verify it's correct?

#### Open Questions
1. The authors paint a promising picture with scalability across i)model sizes and ii) dataset size. Since they fractionize the limited ~16k datapoints that they have to test that hypothesis, I wonder if there's a threshold for dataset size where this framework chokes.
2. What is the justification for why gradient-based steering works? Is it truly because they correspond to meaningful semantic directions?
#### Suggestions
- One way to categorize interpretations could be using confidence levels to quantify the uncertainty of generated interpretations.
