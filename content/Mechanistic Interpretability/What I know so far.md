> *This was written about a month into learning about (mechanistic) interpretability.*
##### Interpretability is a loosely-defined term
I naively used to think interpretability meant explainability, but have since, come to know better. I've learnt about faithfulness and plausibility, various notions of transparency (simulatability, decomposability and algorithmic transparency) and post-hoc interpretability and have come to the conclusion that there is no well-defined universal meaning for AI interpretability. It can mean different things to different people and can change what it means for a single individual based on whatever they're currently working on. But this was actually a freeing conclusion since it now means, I can define it based on the scope of my ongoing work at any given time.
##### I find mechanistic interpretability to still be interesting
Do I only understand half the things I read? Yes.\
Do I get frustrated that it's not beginner friendly? Yes.\
Does it drive me crazy that researchers in this field lay out their phenomenal ideas in verbose posts with an irksome structure? Yes.

Do I still continue to read, understand and attempt at implementing parts of it? Also, yes.

I first got intrigued by mechanistic interpretability precisely because I wanted to learn how neural networks are forming representations and coordinating interactions between atomic components under the hood to arrive at a given prediction. This was an unanswered question that I always had and in the beginning, I thought this field would teach me that. A month into it, I've come to realize everyone working in mech interp is trying to find an answer to this very question. And that's why it's so interesting. Because there's no definite answer. Because conclusions that have been made in the past have been butchered since and newer conclusions seem to be reaching that same fate.
##### What is actionable interpretability?
Honestly, I don't know yet. To me, it feels like the next step after mech interp, almost like an application domain for interpretability. There was a recent *[post](https://www.goodfire.ai/research/interpretability-for-alzheimers-detection#)* from Goodfire AI about understanding DNA sequences using SAEs to understand Alzhiemer's prediction, which I think is closer to what actionable interpretability means, when you can use the techniques and findings from mech interp and extend it to an external domain. This avenue seems interesting, though.
