#### Kernel Methods
The problem: Linear models only draw straight lines, but real data needs curves (is rarely linearly separable)

The fix: Transform data into a richer space where a straight line does the job. This transformation is called a feature map (manually engineer features: if input is $x \in R$, create features $(x,x^2,x^3...)$ to fit a polynomial)

A **feature map** $\phi(x)$ lifts input into a (possibly very high or infinite dimensional) space where the data *is* linearly separable.

We never actually need to compute $\phi(x)$ explicitly. All we ever need is the **inner product** $\langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle$. A **kernel** is just a function that computes this inner product implicitly:
$k(x, x') = \langle \phi(x), \phi(x') \rangle$
This way, we get the expressive power of a rich feature map without ever computing it explicitly. We only ever need to know how similar two points are to each other in that rich space.

##### Kernel Ridge Regression
To predict a new point, look at how similar it is to every training point, and take a weighted combination of their labels. Points more similar to the test point get more influence. 

Basically, the idea behind kernel methods is to make similarity precise and to solve for the weights efficiently,

#### Neural Tangent Kernels
- Neural networks have millions of parameters than data points. Classical theory says they should overfit badly, but they don't. Why?
	- When a network is very wide, gradient descent behaves in a surprisingly simply, predictable way.
		- As the network is trained, its predictions on the training data change with each gradient step (predictions evolve over time during training).
		- The NTK captures how much a gradient step (triggered by loss) at one training point moves the prediction at another training point (NTK captures the geometry of this evolution). 
		- If two inputs are similar, their predictions move together during training.
		- **NTK result: In the infinite-width limit, neural network training (with gradient descent) becomes equivalent to kernel ridge regression.**

- For.a wide two-layer NN initialized randomly:
	- Each entry of the kernel matrix $H(t)$ is an average over $m$ neurons
	- As width $m \rightarrow \infty$, by law of large numbers, this average stops being random and stops at a deterministic value $H^*$.
	- $H^*$ stays essentially constant throughout all trining
