###### Big Picture (contd. from 2)
How tightly does a random quantity concentrate around its expected value?
	- **Markov's inequality** - Very basic, very loose
	- **Chebyshev's inequality** - Uses variance, tighter
	- **Hoeffding's inequality** - Uses boundedness, much tighter
	- **Sub-Gaussian random variables** - Generalizes Hoeffding

#### Hoeffding's Revision
- $\Pr\left[\left|\hat{\mu}_n - \mu\right| > \varepsilon\right] \leq 2e^{-2\varepsilon^2 n}$
- As we collect more samples, the probability of being far off from the truth shrinks _exponentially_ fast. This is much stronger than what Markov or Chebyshev give.
- Three things to notice:
	- **More samples ($n$)** → the exponent gets more negative → probability drops fast
	- **Larger tolerance ($ε$)** → easier to satisfy, probability drops faster
	- **The bound doesn't depend on the true mean $\mu$ — it works regardless of the underlying distribution, as long as the variables are bounded
- Different from Markov's and Chebyshev's because of boundedness

#### Chernoff Bounds for Poisson Trials
##### What's a Poisson Trial?
- An independent random variable (0-1). Either an event happens (1) or it doesn't (0).
- Eg: voters supporting a candidate, coin flip
- Each trial is independent (has its own probability of being one)

##### Hoeffding vs Chernoff
- Hoeffding: Off by $+/- \epsilon$ absolute difference (doesn't care about the true mean)
- Chernoff: Off by a factor $(1+\delta)$ (relative difference to the mean). It gets *sharper when $\mu$ is small*, because a small mean means the distribution is very concentrated near zero.
##### Definition
- Let $X = X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n$ be the total count, and $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X]$ be the expected count.
- The Chernoff bound says:
	- $$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] \leq e^{-\mu\delta^2/3} 
\quad \text{for } 0 < \delta \leq 1
$$
- The probability that a count (X) exceeds the mean ($\mu$) by a factor of $(1+\delta)$ (say 20% more than expected) drops exponentially in $\mu$ and $\delta^2$.

##### Proof

###### Setup
- $n$ independent Poisson trials $X_1, X_2, ... , X_n$ where $Pr[X_i = 1] = p$. 
- Let $X = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$ and $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i$.
	- $\mu = \mathbb{E}[X] = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\right] = \sum_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i$
- We want to bound $\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu]$: probability that total count exceeds its mean by a factor of $(1+\delta)$ (X deviates from its expectation $\mu$ by $\delta \mu$ or more).
- To develop the Chernoff bound, we need to compute MGF of X

###### Step 1: MGF Trick(Markov on the exponent)
- Directly bounding $\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu]$ is hard. So we use the same trick as in Hoeffding: apply Markov's inequality to $e^{tX}$ instead of X directly. 
	- Exponentiating turns the problem into something multiplicative
	- Exponentiation preserves the direction of inequalities and since $e^{tX}$ is non-negative, Markov can be applied
- For any $t > 0$: $$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] = \Pr[e^{tX} \geq e^{t(1+\delta)\mu}] \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]}{e^{t(1+\delta)\mu}}$$
	- Since Markov states: for any non-negative r.v. Z and threshold $t$:
		- $Pr[Z \geq t] \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[Z]}{t}$
- The exponential function amplifies the tails, making the bound tighter. Now, we compute $\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]$, the MGF of X.
###### Step 2: Compute MGF of X
- Since $X_i$ are independent, the MGF of their sum is the product of individual MGFs (property of MGF). $$\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] = \prod_{i=1}^n \mathbb{E}[e^{tX_i}]$$
- Since $X_i$ is 0-1, the MGF of a single $X_i$ is (expectation for a 0-1 r.v.: sum over all possible values, weighted by probabilities): $$\mathbb{E}[e^{tX_i}] = p_i e^{t.1} + e^{t.0}(1 - p_i) = p_i e^t + (1 - p_i) = 1 + p_i(e^t - 1)$$
- Then use the inequality $1 + y \leq e^y$ (true for all $y$), with $y = p_i(e^t - 1)$:
$$1 + p_i(e^t - 1) \leq e^{p_i(e^t - 1)}$$
- Multiplying across all $i$:
$$\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}] \leq \prod_{i=1}^n e^{p_i(e^t-1)} = e^{(e^t - 1)\mu}$$

###### Step 3: Plug Back In (to Step 1)
$$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] = \Pr[e^{tX} \geq e^{t(1+\delta)\mu}] \leq \frac{\mathbb{E}[e^{tX}]}{e^{t(1+\delta)\mu}}$$
Combining with Step 1:
$$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] \leq \frac{e^{(e^t-1)\mu}}{e^{t(1+\delta)\mu}}$$
- Since this holds for any $t>0$, we can make the bound (t)as tight as possible.

###### Step 4: Optimize over $t$
- Set $t = ln(1 + \delta)> 0$, which is the value that minimizes the right hand side. Plugging in:$$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] \leq \left(\frac{e^\delta}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}\right)^\mu$$
###### Step 5: Simplify
- For $0 < \delta \leq 1$: $$\frac{e^\delta}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}} \leq e^{-\delta^2/3}$$
- $f(\delta) = \delta - (1+\delta)ln (1+\delta) + \frac{\delta^2}{3} \leq 0$ (then, take double derivative)
- $f(0) = 0 and f^{'}(\delta) \leq 0$ on [0,1], so f never rises above zero
$$\Pr[X \geq (1+\delta)\mu] \leq e^{-\mu\delta^2/3}$$
